OKC Tree Care and Maintenance Guide
Source: OKC Beautiful (okcbeautiful.com/tree-resources) - Tree Care Guide

== WATERING ==
- Young trees (first 1–3 years): water deeply 3–4 times per week for the first 2–3 weeks after planting.
- After establishment: water once per week during warm seasons (spring, summer, fall).
- Winter watering: every other week. Winter watering helps insulate roots from freezing temperatures.
- Water at the base/root zone of the tree, not the trunk or leaves.
- Use slow, deep soaking for 1–2 minutes at the root base.
- Signs of underwatering: wilting leaves, brown leaf edges, early leaf drop.
- Signs of overwatering: yellowing leaves, root rot, fungal growth.

== MULCHING ==
- Apply 3–4 inches of organic mulch (wood chips, shredded bark) in a wide ring.
- Keep mulch at least 1–2 inches away from the trunk (no volcano mulching — it causes rot).
- Mulch ring should extend to the drip line of the tree canopy if possible.
- Benefits of mulching:
  * Suppresses grass and weed competition for nutrients
  * Retains soil moisture, reduces watering needs
  * Moderates extreme soil temperatures
  * Improves soil structure as it decomposes
  * Prevents soil compaction from foot traffic
- Refresh mulch layer each spring.

== PRUNING ==
- Best time to prune: late winter (January–February) when trees are dormant.
- Pruning in winter promotes strong, healthy new growth in spring.
- Ensures good shape and sturdy structure.
- Never remove more than 25% of a tree's canopy in a single year.
- Pruning goals:
  * Remove dead, damaged, or diseased branches
  * Improve structure and balance
  * Prevent crossing/rubbing branches
  * Clear branches from structures, utilities, or walkways
- Use clean, sharp tools. Disinfect between trees to prevent disease spread.
- Make cuts just outside the branch collar (the swollen area where branch meets trunk).
- Do NOT leave stubs — they invite disease and pests.
- Avoid heavy pruning in summer — it stresses the tree.

== STAKING ==
- Stake only if necessary (high-wind sites, very large transplanted trees).
- Use two stakes placed opposite each other.
- Allow slight trunk movement — this is essential for developing trunk strength and root anchorage.
- Use wide rubber hose or tree straps, not wire directly against bark.
- Remove stakes after 6–8 months. Never leave in place more than 12 months.

== PROTECTING THE TRUNK ==
- Never damage the trunk with lawnmowers or string trimmers.
- Trunk damage impairs the tree's ability to transport water and nutrients.
- Even small wounds can allow disease and insect entry.
- Install a trunk guard or mulch ring around young trees for lawn equipment protection.

== FERTILIZING ==
- Young newly planted trees do NOT need fertilizer immediately — it can burn roots.
- After 1 year, a light application of balanced slow-release fertilizer in early spring can help.
- Over-fertilizing causes rapid, weak growth and increases pest susceptibility.

== COMMON PROBLEMS AND SOLUTIONS ==
- Yellow leaves: possible iron chlorosis (alkaline soils common in OKC), overwatering, or nutrient deficiency.
- Leaf scorch (brown edges): heat stress or underwatering — increase deep watering.
- Premature leaf drop: drought stress, disease, or pest infestation.
- Mushrooms at base: Armillaria root rot — inspect for root health issues.
- Bark cracks: sunscald or frost damage — whitewash trunks of young trees in winter.
