Native Trees of Oklahoma - Oklahoma City Region
Sources: OKC Beautiful, Oklahoma State University Extension, USDA Plants Database

== WHY NATIVE TREES ==
Native Oklahoma trees are adapted to local climate conditions including:
- Hot, dry summers (average high 95°F+ in July–August)
- Cold winters (occasional ice storms and temperatures below 10°F)
- Periodic drought and heavy rains
- Clay and sandy loam soils common in central Oklahoma
- High alkaline soil pH (7.5–8.5) common in OKC area

Native trees require less water, fertilizer, and maintenance than non-native species.
They support local wildlife, birds, and pollinators better than exotic species.

== SMALL TREES (under 25 ft) — Good for small yards, near structures ==

Eastern Redbud (Cercis canadensis)
- Height: 15–25 ft | Spread: 15–25 ft | Diameter: ~20 ft canopy
- Oklahoma's state tree. Brilliant pink/magenta flowers in early spring before leaves emerge.
- Tolerates partial shade and clay soils. Very drought-tolerant once established.
- Great for understory planting under large trees.
- Planting buffer needed: 10 ft from structures, 20 ft canopy at maturity.
- Note: This is one of the 3 tree types in the OKC Tree Planter app.

Rusty Blackhaw (Viburnum rufidulum)
- Height: 10–20 ft | Multi-stemmed shrub/small tree
- White spring flowers, dark blue berries, brilliant red fall color.
- Tolerates shade and dry conditions. Good for woodland edges.

American Plum (Prunus americana)
- Height: 15–25 ft | Thicket-forming
- White spring flowers, small red/yellow edible plums.
- Excellent wildlife value. Tolerates poor soils.

== MEDIUM TREES (25–45 ft) — Good for residential lots ==

Shumard Oak (Quercus shumardii)
- Height: 40–60 ft | Spread: 40–60 ft
- Fast-growing oak with excellent fall color (red/orange/brown).
- Tolerates wet and dry soils. Adaptable to OKC's clay soils.
- Excellent shade tree for residential lots.

Chinkapin Oak (Quercus muehlenbergii)
- Height: 40–55 ft | Spread: 40–50 ft
- Tolerates alkaline, rocky, and dry soils better than most oaks — ideal for OKC limestone soils.
- Good fall color, acorns valuable to wildlife.

Bur Oak (Quercus macrocarpa)
- Height: 60–80 ft | Spread: 60–80 ft | Long-lived (300–400 years)
- Very drought and heat tolerant. Fire-resistant thick bark.
- Extremely adaptable to OKC's climate extremes.

Pecan (Carya illinoinensis) — Oklahoma State Tree
- Height: 70–100 ft | Spread: 40–75 ft
- Produces edible nuts. Needs well-drained, deep soil.
- Long-lived. Requires large space — not for small lots.

== LARGE TREES (over 45 ft) — Require significant space ==

Eastern Red Cedar (Juniperus virginiana)
- Height: 40–50 ft (can reach 90 ft) | Evergreen
- Very drought and wind tolerant. Excellent windbreak.
- Dense year-round cover for birds. Berry-like cones eaten by Cedar Waxwings.
- Pioneer species — widely adapted to OKC soils.

Loblolly Pine (Pinus taeda) — non-native but common in OKC
- Height: 60–90 ft | Spread: 20–35 ft
- Evergreen, fast-growing. Good for screening.
- Prefers well-drained, acidic soils.
- Note: This is the "Pine" tree type in the OKC Tree Planter app (40 ft diameter).

Post Oak (Quercus stellata)
- Height: 35–60 ft | Slow-growing
- Cross-shaped leaves. Very drought tolerant.
- Common on rocky, sandy soils in central Oklahoma.

== APP TREE TYPES — Specific Details ==

Redbud (Cercis canadensis)
- OKC Tree Planter app uses 20 ft diameter for buffer calculation.
- Ideal for small yards, understory planting, near structures.
- Planting season: fall (October–November) preferred.
- Water: moderate, drought tolerant once established (2–3 years).

Oak (Generic large oak — Shumard, Chinkapin, or Bur in OKC)
- OKC Tree Planter app uses 60 ft diameter for buffer calculation.
- Requires large open space — not appropriate near buildings.
- Long-lived investment. Increases property value significantly.
- Plant away from structures, utility lines, and hardscapes.

Pine (Loblolly or Eastern White Pine in OKC context)
- OKC Tree Planter app uses 40 ft diameter for buffer calculation.
- Evergreen provides year-round screening and wind protection.
- Avoid planting in areas with poor drainage or high alkaline clay soil.
- Roots can be aggressive near foundations over time.

== OKC CLIMATE SUMMARY (affects species selection) ==
- USDA Hardiness Zone: 7a–7b (avg winter min 0°F to 10°F)
- Average annual rainfall: ~36 inches (highly variable — drought years below 20 inches)
- Summer heat: frequently 95–105°F in July–August
- Clay-heavy soils common in OKC metro — choose species tolerant of poor drainage
- Ice storms: occasional heavy ice (January–February) — avoid brittle-wood species (silver maple, Bradford pear)
