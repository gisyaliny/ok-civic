OKC Tree Planting Guide - Oklahoma City, Oklahoma
Source: OKC Beautiful (okcbeautiful.com/tree-resources)

== PROPER TREE PLANTING ==

Step 1: Choose the Right Location
- Check overhead utilities before planting any tree. Never plant a large tree under power lines.
- Ensure the location has enough space for the tree's mature canopy diameter.
- Check zoning requirements. Contact OKC Planning at okc.gov for specific zoning rules.
- Avoid planting within building setbacks or over utility easements.
- Consider the tree's mature height and spread when selecting a site.

Step 2: Choose the Right Tree
- Match the tree species to the site conditions: sun exposure, soil drainage, space available.
- Native Oklahoma trees are better adapted to the local climate and require less maintenance.
- OKC Beautiful recommends checking the Native Trees of Oklahoma list for best species.
- For street trees, check OKC's approved street tree list before planting.

Step 3: Digging the Hole
- Dig a hole 2–3 times wider than the root ball but only as deep as the root ball height.
- A shallow, wide hole is better than a deep, narrow one.
- The top of the root ball should sit at or slightly above ground level.
- Loosen the soil around the hole so roots can spread easily.

Step 4: Planting the Tree
- Remove all packaging, burlap, and wire basket from the root ball before planting.
- Remove synthetic burlap completely. Natural burlap can be folded back but not left over the root ball.
- Place the tree in the center of the hole with the trunk flare visible above grade.
- Backfill with the original soil. Do not amend with fertilizer at planting time.
- Tamp soil gently to remove air pockets.

Step 5: Watering After Planting
- Water deeply immediately after planting to settle the soil.
- For the first 2–3 weeks, water 3–4 times per week.
- After the establishment period (2–3 weeks), water once per week during warm months.
- Water every other week in winter.
- Deep, infrequent watering is better than shallow, frequent watering.
- Apply water at the root zone, not the trunk.

Step 6: Mulching
- Apply 3–4 inches of mulch around the tree in a wide ring.
- Pull mulch back 1–2 inches from the trunk (no "volcano mulching").
- Mulch suppresses weeds, retains moisture, and moderates soil temperature.
- Refresh mulch each spring.

Step 7: Staking
- Stake young trees only if necessary (exposed, windy sites or large trees).
- Use two stakes on opposite sides of the tree.
- Allow some trunk movement — this builds root and trunk strength.
- Remove stakes after 6–8 months. Never leave stakes longer than 1 year.
- Use wide rubber straps or hose material to protect the bark.

== PLANTING SEASON ==
- Best time to plant trees in Oklahoma City: October through April (fall through early spring).
- Spring planting (March–April) is good but requires more watering through summer.
- Fall planting (October–November) gives roots time to establish before summer heat.
- Avoid planting in summer unless irrigation is readily available.

== TREE SIZE AND SPACING REQUIREMENTS ==
- Small trees (< 25 ft tall): plant at least 5 ft from curb, 10 ft from structures.
- Medium trees (25–45 ft): plant at least 8 ft from curb, 15 ft from structures.
- Large trees (> 45 ft): plant at least 10 ft from curb, 20–30 ft from structures.
- Always verify spacing with OKC zoning and utility regulations.

== OKC BEAUTIFUL FREE TREES PROGRAM ==
- Tree OKC program provides free trees to homeowners, schools, businesses, and community organizations.
- Planting season: October through April.
- Since 2022, Tree OKC has planted and distributed more than 5,000 trees and seedlings.
- To request trees: submit a general tree request form at okcbeautiful.com.
- Contact: Michael Payne, michael@okcbeautiful.com, 405-525-8822.
- Community Foresters Program: annual 4-week educational initiative every Saturday in February.
  25 individuals learn from local professionals about tree care, identification, planting, pruning.
