OKC Zoning and Tree Planting Rules
Sources: OKC Planning Department, OKC Zoning Code, OKC Beautiful

== OKC ZONING BASICS FOR TREE PLANTING ==

The OKC Tree Planter app checks three spatial constraints before recommending a planting site:
1. Zoning compliance — the point must fall within an appropriate zoning area.
2. Building footprint conflict — the tree's canopy buffer must not intersect buildings.
3. Tree canopy overlap — the buffer should not overlap existing tree canopy (optional check).

== COMMON OKC ZONING CLASSES ==

RS (Residential Single-Family):
- RS-1: Low density, large lots (20,000+ sq ft). Good for large oak/pecan trees.
- RS-2: Medium density (10,000+ sq ft lots). Good for medium and small trees.
- RS-3: Medium-high density (7,000+ sq ft lots). Prefer smaller trees near structures.
- RS-4: Higher density residential. Space is limited; choose small to medium trees.
- RS-5, RS-6, RS-7: High density. Street trees and yard trees require careful spacing.

RM (Residential Multi-Family):
- Higher density; tree planting typically limited to landscaping setbacks.

C (Commercial):
- Commercial zones may have specific landscaping requirements.
- Trees must not obstruct sight lines or signage per OKC code.
- Commercial parking lots: OKC requires 1 tree per 10 parking spaces in many districts.

O (Office):
- Similar to commercial; landscaping buffers often required.

I (Industrial):
- Limited tree planting opportunities; buffers required between industrial and residential uses.

PUD (Planned Unit Development):
- Trees governed by specific development plan. Check with OKC Planning.

== SETBACK RULES FOR TREES ==
These are general guidelines; always verify with OKC Planning Department:
- Minimum distance from building foundation: 10–15 ft for small trees, 20–30 ft for large trees.
- Minimum distance from property line: typically 5 ft (check specific zone).
- Minimum distance from curb/street edge: 3–5 ft for small trees, 5–8 ft for large trees.
- Minimum distance from underground utilities: 5–10 ft lateral clearance.
- Minimum distance from water/sewer lines: 10 ft (tree roots can damage pipes).
- Power lines overhead: do not plant tall trees within 20 ft of power lines.
- Traffic sight triangles at intersections: no trees taller than 3 ft within 30 ft of intersection.

== WHY THE APP SHOWS "OUTSIDE KNOWN ZONING AREAS" ==
If the suitability result says "Location is outside known zoning areas," it means:
- The clicked point falls in an area not covered by the OKC Zoning dataset.
- This can happen in unincorporated areas outside OKC city limits.
- It may also indicate a data gap or an un-zoned parcel.
- Try using Identify mode to click directly on a colored zoning polygon to see the zone.
- The zoning layer may be blocked by CORS if running from localhost. Deploy to app.okc.gov for full access.

== BUILDING FOOTPRINT CONFLICTS ==
If the result says "Conflict with existing building footprint":
- The tree's canopy buffer (radius = half the tree's diameter) overlaps a building footprint.
- This does NOT mean the tree is at the building — it means the mature canopy would reach it.
- Solutions:
  * Move the planting point further from the building (try 10–30 ft away).
  * Choose a smaller tree (Redbud at 20 ft vs Oak at 60 ft).
  * Use the building direction hint (N/S/E/W) to know which direction to move.

== TREE CANOPY OVERLAP ==
If the result shows a canopy conflict:
- The proposed planting buffer overlaps existing tree canopy in the raster layer.
- This suggests there may already be a tree at or near this location.
- You can still plant a new tree nearby — look for an open gap in the canopy layer.
- Toggle the Tree Canopy layer in the sidebar to see canopy coverage visually.
